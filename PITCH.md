# MotoKE — the dealer pitch

Everything needed to walk into a yard and hold a conversation. Read the first two
sections before a meeting; the rest is there for when they push back.

---

## 1. The problem, in one sentence

**A Kenyan dealership loses most of its sales not because the customer disliked the car,
but because the finance fell over — and nobody could see it happening.**

Unpack that and there are four separate leaks.

### Leak 1 — the customer never finds out what they can afford

They browse cars by price. Price is the wrong number: almost nobody pays cash. They fall
for a 4M car, apply, get declined, and go quiet. Nothing was wrong with the car. The
deposit was wrong, or the term, or the lender.

**What it costs you:** every declined application is a customer who was ready to buy and
walked away embarrassed. They rarely come back.

### Leak 2 — one lender, one answer, one shot

Most yards send the customer to whichever bank they know best. If that bank says no, the
deal dies. But a bank saying no is one institution's rules — a sacco that takes 10% down
might have said yes on the same file.

**What it costs you:** deals that were fundable, lost to the wrong first phone call.

### Leak 3 — the application disappears

The customer applies, then hears nothing. They ring on day three. And day five. Somebody
has to stop what they're doing, find the file, and reassure them. Then the customer rings
again on day eight.

**What it costs you:** a fortnight of phone calls per deal, and a customer whose
confidence drains a little more each time they have to chase you.

### Leak 4 — the stock quietly eats the margin

A car on the forecourt is borrowed money that's depreciating. Interest plus depreciation
runs at roughly **30% a year** — about **KES 2,500 a month on a 1M car**, whether anyone
looks at it or not. Most yards don't know which cars have been sitting, or which are slow
because they're overpriced rather than unpopular.

**What it costs you:** margin, silently, on the cars you least want to be holding.

## 2. What MotoKE is

**A car sales site with a real asset-finance engine behind it.**

Not a brochure with a "call us" button. The customer works out their budget, compares
every lender on your panel on the actual car, applies, uploads documents, and follows the
application to delivery — without a single phone call. Your team works the same deal from
a console that shows exactly where every file sits and what stock is costing you.

The difference from everything else in this market:

> **They list cars and hope. You close finance and can prove it.**

## 4. Why it's a must-have, not a nice-to-have

Four arguments, in the order they usually land.

### It makes you money on deals you are already losing

You don't need more traffic. You need to close more of what walks in. Comparing lenders
instead of guessing, and restructuring instead of accepting a decline, converts deals that
are dying today.

**The line:** *"How many applications did you lose to a decline last month? What if half
of those had a lender who'd have said yes?"*

### It saves the money already leaking out of the yard

Stock ageing tells you which cars are costing you and which are overpriced. On a
sixty-car forecourt that is millions a year in interest and depreciation on stock that
should have been repriced weeks ago.

**The line:** *"Which car has been on your lot longest? What has it cost you to keep?"*
Most principals cannot answer. That is the pitch.

### It removes work

The tracker ends the chasing calls. Test-drive booking ends the callback. Lending research
found firms that automate status tracking see **30–50% fewer post-submission calls**.

**The line:** *"How many times a day does someone ring to ask where their application is?"*

### It is the trust play, in a market that runs on trust

Kenyan buyers are frightened of used cars, and rightly — fake logbooks, undisclosed
logbook loans, wound-back odometers. MotoKE puts those checks on the page and says plainly
when something has **not** been checked.

Volunteering the number that makes a car look expensive is the most credible thing a
dealer can do. **Nobody else here does it.**

## 6. The ten-minute demo

Run it in this order. It builds.

1. **"What can I afford?"** — income 150,000. A real range appears, and cars in it.
   *This is the conversation you have twenty times a week, done in ten seconds.*
2. **Open a car.** Price check badge, provenance block, 42-point condition report.
   *This is what stops the haggling and the doubt.*
3. **Compare financing.** Ten lenders on that car. Point at True APR.
   *That lender is charging 24%, not 13%. Your customer cannot see that anywhere else.*
4. **Running costs.** *Owning this car costs another 78% of its price over five years. If
   nobody tells them, they find out the hard way — and they miss payments.*
5. **Apply**, then **track it**. Show the step rail and "waiting on you".
   *That is the phone call you never have to take again.*
6. **Console → Stock ageing.** Point at the repricing list.
   *These nine cars are slow because of their price, not the market. This one has cost you
   180,000 to keep.*

Close on step 6. It is the one aimed at the person signing the cheque.

## 7. Objections, and honest answers

**"My customers aren't online."**
They are on WhatsApp, and every page has a WhatsApp button pre-filled with the car and the
reference. The site does the qualifying; the closing still happens on the phone and in the
yard.

**"We already have a website."**
You have a catalogue. Ask it what a customer earning 150,000 can afford, or which lender
approves them, or where their application is. This is the difference between a brochure
and a sales tool.

**"The banks won't give us their rates."**
They publish them. Every lender, rate and fee is editable in the console. Start with the
two or three you already use and add the rest as you go.

**"How long to set up?"**
Your stock, branding and lender panel, and it runs. Photographs are the long pole — bring
your own or use licensed renders.

**"What if it's wrong about a rate?"**
Every figure comes from rules you control, and the site says a quote is indicative until
the lender confirms. Nothing is presented as a credit decision.

## 8. What is honestly not built yet

Say these before they find them. It buys more credibility than it costs.

- **M-Pesa is a faithful simulator** until Daraja credentials are added — a day's work.
- **Submitting to a lender is a status change**, not an API call. Kenyan lenders mostly
  don't offer one; your team still sends the pack.
- **CRB is self-declared.** The lender does the real check.
- **No SMS gateway.** Codes print to the console; wire Africa's Talking when needed.
- **The demo lenders and rates are fictional.** Replace with real published terms before
  any commercial conversation.
- **Photography.** The demo uses generated showroom images. A live yard needs its own.

## 9. Where the numbers come from

Worth being able to answer, because a sharp principal will ask.

- **Fuel** — live EPRA maximum retail prices for your town, refreshed automatically, with
  the age shown on screen. Stale is visible, never silently wrong.
- **Servicing** — parts and labour by badge tier: a BMW is not a Toyota.
- **Resale** — retention by make in the Kenyan market. Toyota holds hardest; the German
  and British marques fall fastest.
- **Instalments** — reducing balance and flat handled separately and correctly, APR solved
  from the real cash flows.
- **Specs** — measured where you have the logbook, modelled where you don't, **and the page
  says which**.

The rule throughout: **an estimate is never dressed up as a fact**, and a check nobody ran
never reads as a pass.
